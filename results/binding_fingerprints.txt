Binding-fingerprint targets (P8.1)
======================================================================

koff = kon * Ki at every receptor target; the Ki fixes the ratio and
neither rate, so the kon column is where the assumption lives.

borrowed-from-subtype (1)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  MOR          kon = 0.144 nM^-1 min^-1
      density gene: Oprm1
      diprenorphine's measured association rate at KAPPA (opioid.KAPPA_KON_DIPRENORPHINE), used at mu because no mu association rate is available. This is not a new decision: opioid._ligand already falls back to exactly this value for every mu ligand it builds, and the fallback is reused here rather than replaced so that a fingerprint ligand and a hand-built one at mu share one timescale.
      note: The site is scenarios.mu_opioid_receptor_site_calibrated (Track A: fentanyl [Mann2022]/[Volpe2011], naloxone [Mann2022]). See docs/architecture.md, P8.1.

assumed (1)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  GABA_A_BZD   kon = 0.2 nM^-1 min^-1
      density gene: Gabra1 (proxy)
      scenarios.gaba_a_benzodiazepine_ligands_calibrated's `kon_shared`, which that function's own comment flags as 'assumed, not measured'. Read off the ligand rather than restated so the two cannot drift.
      gene: PROXY: the benzodiazepine site is an interface between an alpha and the gamma2 subunit of a pentamer. Gabra1 is the most abundant alpha subunit and genes.py already flags that no single gene is the GABA-A receptor; alpha4 and alpha6 assemblies carry NO benzodiazepine site at all, so this map over-counts.
      note: `efficacy` at this site is REPURPOSED as potentiation strength, not channel activation, because the site acts through connection_modulation (a benzodiazepine gates nothing itself). A fingerprint's efficacy for this target therefore has to be a potentiation figure, and 1.0 means 'as strong as the diazepam the P2.2 gain was fitted with'.

inexact (2): MOR/fentanyl, MOR/naloxone

Every row above reproduces its ligand's AFFINITY exactly; the two
inexact rows are mu-opioid, where scenarios.py derived each kon from
that ligand's own MEASURED koff [Mann2022] and a fingerprint has only
the Ki. What that costs, in the quantity the measurement was made
for:

  fentanyl  receptor residence time 1/koff: 3.85 min measured, 5.16 min from the fingerprint, 1.3x too long
  naloxone  receptor residence time 1/koff: 0.42 min measured, 4.57 min from the fingerprint, 10.9x too long

Naloxone is the one that matters: scenarios.py's own note says it is
naloxone's SHORT receptor residence time, not its clearance, that
makes reversal wear off before a large fentanyl exposure does. A
fingerprint-derived naloxone holds the receptor almost eleven times
too long, so a reversal timing predicted from binding data alone
would be wrong in exactly the direction that matters clinically.

A worked prediction: diazepam 2 mg/kg IP, rat
==============================================================================

Fingerprint: one row, Ki 3.6 nM at the benzodiazepine site
             (Mohler & Okada, Life Sci 1977;20:2101), efficacy 1.0
             supplied separately because no binding database has one.
PK:          DosedOneCompartmentPK(route='IP', Vd/F=19.3 L/kg, CL/F=255 mL/min/kg, F folded in (apparent parameters))
Partition:   receptor-drivable
             Kp 4.5 +/- 0.1 and f(u,plasma) 0.196 from [Friedman1986] (rat, 5 mg/kg IP); f(u,brain) 0.04 from [Gustafsson2019] Table 2 (Sprague-Dawley rat, whole_brain, brain-homogenate equilibrium dialysis)
Compartment: unbound brain

  peak unbound brain concentration  52.23 nM
  peak benzodiazepine-site occupancy 0.936 at 17 min
  site activation at peak            0.936

This is where P8.1 stops. Turning that activation into an anxiety
score needs the potentiation gain P2.2 fitted and a region-wise
density, which is P8.2's composition and not a binding fingerprint's
to supply.
