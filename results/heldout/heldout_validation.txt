4. What the model can reach, over ALL gains
========================================================================
The endpoint index saturates, so there is a largest effect the model can
produce at each endpoint for ANY gain whatsoever. An observation outside
this interval is refuted by the model's structure rather than by a
parameter choice. Convergence of each range is checked rather than
assumed; 'drift' is how much the index still moved over the outermost
decade of the sweep.

7. Monotonicity: the test no gain can pass
========================================================================
Every stage from dose to endpoint index is monotone, so the model cannot
produce a dose-response that rises and then falls, at any gain, at any
target, with any density map. A published series that turns over refutes
the model outright. Whether each turnover survives its own error bars is
computed at two thresholds, because reporting only the threshold that
passes would be choosing the test after seeing the data.

  alprazolam / anxiety
    doses  0.1, 0.3, 1
    ratios 0.9851+/-0.0574, 0.8723+/-0.0530, 0.9864+/-0.0739
    turns over: True
      at 1.00 sd: REFUTES
      at 1.96 sd: not resolved
    turns over, and the turnover exceeds 1 sd on both the rising and the
    falling step: NO gain reproduces this



8. The occupancy tier, and why it could not be run
========================================================================
Occupancy is the one prediction with NO unfitted parameter in it: PK, the
partition and the Ki fix it completely. It is therefore the tier whose
absence costs most, and what blocks it is named per compound rather than
summarised.

  alprazolam: 1 published occupancy observation(s), blocked by pharmacokinetics, brain_partition
      0.9 mg/kg IP mouse -> 50% at GABA_A_BZD [Cho1986]

  caffeine: 2 published occupancy observation(s), blocked by pharmacokinetics, brain_partition
      1 mg/kg IP mouse -> 32% at A2A [ElYacoubi2001]
      6.25 mg/kg IP mouse -> 66% at A2A [ElYacoubi2001]

  cocaine: 2 published occupancy observation(s), blocked by pharmacokinetics, brain_partition
      0.25 mg/kg IV mouse -> 50% at DAT [Gatley1999]
      1 mg/kg IV mouse -> 80% at DAT [Gatley1999]

5 occupancy observations were collected and NONE could be scored, because
no held-out compound has both a dose-to-concentration model and a
plasma-to-brain-unbound partition.


9. Searched and empty
========================================================================
21 documented searches that came back empty, and 1 gaps that were NOT
searched. The two are counted separately because presenting an unrun
search as a negative result would be the most consequential overclaim
available in this task.

  alprazolam
    [SEARCHED] rodent dose-to-concentration pharmacokinetics for
    alprazolam: a volume of distribution and a clearance in the same
    species and route
        No usable dose-to-concentration model exists in any accessible
        source. This is the decisive gap for alprazolam and it is the
        exact mirror image of cocaine's: cocaine has a complete rat IV
        disposition and no brain partition, alprazolam has a complete
        brain partition and no disposition. Neither compound can be
        simulated from a dose, for opposite reasons.

    [SEARCHED] a fitted rodent dose-occupancy curve for the
    benzodiazepine site
        Only the single mouse ex vivo ED50 recorded above. A further
        complication is documented and replicated: four independent mouse
        studies ([Miller1987] PMID 2889803, [Mosaddeghi1992] PMID 1331409,
        [Burke1994] PMID 7913233, [Kaplan1990] PMID 2167493) report that
        LOW-dose alprazolam paradoxically INCREASES in vivo
        benzodiazepine-site radioligand binding rather than displacing it,
        which [Miller1987] attributes to an increase in receptor number.
        Any monotone occupancy function fitted to alprazolam will be wrong
        below about 0.1 mg/kg IP in the mouse, and this model's occupancy
        function is monotone by construction.

  amphetamine
