## Reading

**Aripiprazole: a pass, and a fairly clean one.** Kim 2012's plasma EC50,
fitted in Seoul on single oral doses, predicts Ito 2012's Chiba single-dose
group means within 0.051 everywhere, with bias near zero. The regimens match:
both are single oral doses in healthy young men, both use [11C]raclopride, and
both measure parent drug with dehydroaripiprazole reported separately. That
makes this the best-matched comparison in the ledger. The part with teeth is
the 3 mg group, where the hyperbola is steepest. There the prediction is low
in both regions, by 3 and 5 points. Ito scanned at 3.5 h post-dose, before the
plasma-to-occupancy hysteresis Kim measured has fully played out. Hysteresis
on the rising limb would make occupancy *lower* than the plasma EC50 predicts,
the opposite of what is observed, so the 3 mg residual is not explained by
that.

**Ziprasidone: a large miss on one subject.** Suzuki's 204 nM (84 ng/mL)
predicts 35% occupancy at 44.6 ng/mL. Bench measured 77%. The error is 42
occupancy points, four times the pass threshold. Things that count against
over-reading this:

- n=1, flagged anecdotal by the pre-registered rule.
- **Regimen mismatch, flagged on the row**: one 40 mg dose in a healthy
  volunteer, scanned about 5.75 h post-dose. Suzuki fitted steady state in
  patients on 60 mg twice daily with food.
- Bench 1993's occupancy reference is a single placebo subject. The same
  subject reads 77% against the seven-normal mean too, so the reference choice
  does not move it.

Equally, the miss is not small. Read in the constant's own terms, a hyperbola
through Bench's point (Emax fixed at 1) needs EC50 = 44.6 × 0.23/0.77 =
13.3 ng/mL, about 6.3-fold below Suzuki's 84. P21.2 had already recorded that
published ziprasidone constants span 5.5-fold: de Greef 2011 15.4, Uchida 2011
32.9 (fitted Emax), Suzuki 84. This point sits at the low end of that span. It
is evidence that the spread P21.2 recorded is real between studies, not noise
around one value. Those other constants were **not** used to predict Bench's
point. The pre-registration fixes "X's registered constant", and neither
pool's study list can be shown to exclude Bench 1993.

## How the scored observations were found eligible

Each eligible observation meets all three pre-registered criteria, and the
reason is recorded on the row in `OBSERVATIONS`:

1. **Ito 2012** (PLoS One 7:e46488, PMC3460902, full text). Table 1 prints
   occupancy by dose group as mean ± SD and range, for caudate and putamen.
   The Results print parent aripiprazole plasma "averaged between the start
   and end of each scanning" for the [11C]raclopride scan, which pairs it
   with the same visit. For the n=2 9 mg group, Ito prints only ranges
   (39.6–40.4 ng/mL, 75–77%, 77–79%). With two subjects, the endpoints *are*
   the two values, so their midpoint is the group mean exactly. It is not an
   estimate. Disjointness: Chiba (NIRS) volunteers vs Kim 2012's Seoul
   National University Hospital volunteers.
2. **Bench 1993 + Bench 1996** (Psychopharmacology 112:308 and 124:141, both
   read in full from pure.mpg.de). Bench 1996 Table 1 lists subject "(8)",
   footnoted as the "Additional subject from previous dose-finding study
   (Bench et al. 1993) who received 40 mg ziprasidone". It prints a serum
   concentration "at time of PET" of 44.6 ng/ml and BP 0.51. Bench 1993
   Table 1 subject 7 matches on age 34, weight 66 kg, dose 40 mg and BP 0.51
   (±0.03), and prints occupancy 77% against both references. 44.6 ng/ml is
   inside the assay's stated 0.5–50 ng/ml range. Disjointness: London
   volunteers in 1992–95 vs Suzuki's Toronto patients (NCT00818298).

Two data sets that look usable and were **not** used:

- **Bench 1996's other six subjects** print BP and serum but no occupancy.
  Converting BP to occupancy needs a reference BP. The paper leaves three
  open: placebo subject 2.67, nine normals 2.26, age-corrected 2.57. On
  subject 2, the choice moves occupancy by about 9 points. P19.3 refused the
  same kind of conversion for Xiberas 2001, and it is refused here.
- **Bench 1993's seven dosed subjects** print occupancy against serum
  **AUC(0–∞)** in ng·h/ml, not a concentration. That fails criterion 1.

## Searched and empty, per drug

The full list of sources opened and the reason each one failed is
`SEARCHED_AND_EMPTY` in the module. In summary:

- **Haloperidol.** Every disjoint candidate is paywalled with the paired
  numbers absent from the abstract: Wolkin 1989, Nordström 1992, Farde 1988,
  Lim 2013, Reimold 2007, Arakawa 2010. Or it prints no paired plasma (Kessler
  2005), a binding index (Schlösser 1997, Xiberas 2001) or free plasma
  (Videbæk 2001). The other Toronto series (Kapur 1996 and 2000) cannot be
  shown disjoint from Kapur 1997 and Fitzgerald 2000. Lim 2013's abstract
  prints a disjoint fitted EC50 of 0.791 ng/mL (putamen). That is 1.12× the
  repository's 0.708, but it is a constant, not an observation, so it is
  recorded and not scored.
- **Olanzapine.** de Greef 2011's olanzapine pool is printed (its Table II):
  Kapur 1999 (17 points) and Nyberg 1997 (3 points). So disjointness *can*
  be established, which P22.2's olanzapine notes had left open. The one
  readable disjoint candidate is Ismail 2022 (PMC9197923), Table 1:
  36.01 ng/mL, 65.65% occupancy, n=20. It fails same-visit pairing. Its mean
  concentration matches neither visit mean in Nakajima 2016 (PMC4681559),
  which is the same cohort: 61.0 ng/mL before and 42.3 after dose reduction.
  So the concentration and occupancy means are not shown to come from the
  same scans. Graff-Guerrero 2015 pools olanzapine and risperidone patients
  in its occupancy figures.
- **Risperidone (active moiety).** Ito 2009 (PMC6665007) prints occupancy by
  dose but active-moiety plasma only pooled across all twelve subjects.
  Nakajima 2016 prints observed occupancy only in a figure. Ismail 2022 fails
  as for olanzapine. Gefvert 2005's abstract prints per-dose *ranges* of
  occupancy and plasma, which are not pairs.
- **Clozapine.** Nordström 1995, Kapur 1999 and Kessler 2006 hold the paired
  values in closed tables. The abstracts print only ranges.
- **Amisulpride.** la Fougère 2005 and Meisenzahl 2008 report correlations;
  their tables are closed. Xiberas 2001 is a binding index.
- **Paliperidone.** NCT00934635's posted results are one terminated subject
  with occupancy 0 and no plasma. No other parent-paliperidone human PET study
  disjoint from Arakawa 2008 was found.
- **Quetiapine.** See the correction below. On the observation side,
  Vernaleken 2010 prints a per-subject serum and occupancy table (its
  Table 1), but the HTML omits the table and the PDF sits behind a bot check.
  It was not read, and the check was not circumvented. It would have been
  cross-tracer ([18F]fallypride) anyway.

## A correction to P21.2 and to the pre-registration's expectation

P21.2 recorded, and the pre-registration repeated, that no plasma EC50 exists
for quetiapine, "structurally". **That is wrong.** Nord 2011 (Int J
Neuropsychopharmacol 14:1357, PMC3198174, open) fits occupancy against
plasma with "Occ max … fixed to 100%". It reports "K i,app was calculated to
be 1369 nmol/l" for parent quetiapine, pooled over IR and XR, putamen,
[11C]raclopride, healthy controls. IR alone gives 1373 and XR alone 1365; the
active moiety gives 2004 nmol/l. Mathematically that is an Emax-fixed plasma
EC50. P21.2's mechanistic point still stands. Occupancy falls to single
digits at trough while plasma is still measurable, and a direct-effect
hyperbola fitted across peak and trough scans absorbs that hysteresis. It is
a reason to distrust the constant, not evidence that none exists. The
expectation the pre-registration declared ("quetiapine is expected
searched-and-empty") still came true, but for a different reason: the
observations are unreadable, not the constant. The constant is recorded in
the quetiapine row and is not ingested, because there is nothing for it to
predict.

## Deviations from pre-registration

- **D1. EC50 rows for aripiprazole and ziprasidone are held in the P22.3
  module, not the chain registry.** The pre-registration says "X's registered
  constant". At run time the registry held only haloperidol and olanzapine.
  P22.2's dependency note expected P22.2a to ingest the P21.2 drugs, and it
  ingested only olanzapine. The constants used are the P21.2 primary values,
  as P21.2 tabulated them before the pre-registration was written. They were
  re-read here from Kim 2012 and Suzuki 2013 and match. They are kept out of
  `D2_PLASMA_EC50` because that registry drives P22.2's refusal attribution,
  and adding rows there would rewrite a published result. The search results for
  observations were seen before these rows were coded. The rule (P21.2's
  primary row, re-verified) left no choice open to bend to them, and the
  ziprasidone row was kept after it produced a 42-point miss. Ziprasidone's headline striatal value was used rather than a
  subregion because Bench prints "striatum".
- **D2. Region choice when the observation splits striatum.** The
  pre-registration does not say what to do when the EC50 source is
  whole-striatum and the observation source prints caudate and putamen
  separately (Ito 2012). Both regions are scored as observations, and the
  pre-registered metric (MAE over same-tracer observations) is taken over
  both. Per-region MAEs and the independent-group count are reported beside
  it. Neither region alone changes the verdict: caudate 0.0200, putamen
  0.0288.
- **D3. No git-ordering proof between transcription and scoring.** This
  session's diff is applied by the host as one patch, so the commit-order
  proof P22.2 had is not available. Commitment C1 is met in substance. No
  constant, region rule or threshold was changed after any error was seen,
  and the ziprasidone miss is reported at its full size.
