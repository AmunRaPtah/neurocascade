# P22.5: scope and failure analysis for the multi-drug chain

This document follows the standard set by P8.3 (`docs/heldout-failure-analysis.md`)
and P19.7 (`docs/p19-7-scope-and-failure-analysis.md`): state what was tested,
attribute each failure to a layer rather than to the model as a whole, and say
what data would repair it. P19.7 covered the haloperidol-only chain. This one
covers what Phases 21 and 22 added: a chain keyed by drug, nine census drugs,
and ledger rows V5 to V11.

Every number below is regenerated by
`neurocascade.human.multidrug_failure_analysis` (commitment C4 of the
pre-registration). The two tables are the module's rendered output, pasted
whole. `tests/test_multidrug_failure_analysis.py` fails if either table stops
matching the code, so a change upstream that moves a number also breaks this
document. Numbers quoted in the prose are taken from those two tables or from
`regenerated_numbers()`, and the test pins them.

## Result in one paragraph

Of the nine drugs in the Phase 22 census, the chain can drive two end to end
from an oral dose: haloperidol and olanzapine. Seven stop at the PK layer. Two
of those seven, risperidone and aripiprazole, are also refused earlier, at the
analyte layer. Where a comparison against an outside number could be run, the
occupancy hyperbola passes when the EC50 source and the test match on tracer
and regimen (aripiprazole, MAE 0.0244 over 3 independent groups). It misses
badly when they do not (ziprasidone, one subject, error −0.4238). The
whole-chain label test passes 2 of 2 but cannot tell EC50 candidates apart.
For clozapine the chain predicts occupancy correctly, and that prediction
falsifies the occupancy-threshold hypothesis. **No failure here is attributed
to the model as a whole.** Each one sits in a named layer: the PK source
corpus, the analyte basis, the published constants, the regimen, the lack of
an effect compartment, the transducer form, or the hypothesis that links
occupancy to response. Two rows are not model failures at all: the ground
truth was either too sparse or too wide to test against.

## What was tested, by layer

- **pk, dose to plasma.** V5 compared 13 FDA-implied terminal half-lives
  against label half-lives. That checks the PK sources, not the chain's
  output. For the two drug-specific PK sets the chain does drive, there is no
  observation pairing an oral dose with an occupancy (haloperidol 0; olanzapine
  has no registered PET set). So the dose-to-plasma arm is **still untested
  against occupancy for every drug**, as P19.7 found for haloperidol alone.
- **analyte.** V6 (risperidone/paliperidone equipotency) and V7 (aripiprazole
  exposure ratio). Both are cross-source checks of metabolite assumptions, and
  both fail.
- **occupancy constant.** V9 and V10 (P22.3 leave-one-drug-out) test other
  groups' constants on concentrations nobody here chose. V1 to V3 (P19.6)
  remain the haloperidol tests.
- **whole chain.** V8 (P22.2) compares the predicted dose window with the
  label dose range for the two drivable drugs.
- **occupancy to clinical outcome.** V11 (P22.4), run in the concentration
  domain because clozapine has no PK.

No layer downstream of occupancy (effect-site kinetics, transduction, circuit,
behaviour) has been tested against anything, for any drug.

How to read it:

- The `exposure` column is a gate that P22.2's refusal attribution never
  checks. `label_dose_prediction.refusal_layers` tests active-metabolite, pk
  and occupancy. The chain's `_require_no_brain_partition_step` also refuses
  any drug with no Kp,uu record. Today this changes nothing, because the seven
  drugs with no Kp,uu record are exactly the seven refused at pk (a test
  asserts it). But a PK repair alone would expose it. Closing it needs no
  measurement, only a `not-required` record entered with the drug's EC50, as
  P22.2a did for olanzapine.
- `occupancy constant` records where the constant used in Phase 22 is held.
  Only haloperidol and olanzapine are in the chain's registry. The aripiprazole,
  ziprasidone and clozapine rows sit in the P22.3 and P22.4 modules, for the C1
  reasons those documents give. So "the chain has an EC50" and "Phase 22
  tested an EC50" are different sets.

## What this analysis adds to the earlier records

**1. V11's headline margin is cross-tracer.** P22.4's primary constant
(Gründer 2006 putamen) was measured with [18F]fallypride. The 0.65 and 0.78
thresholds it is compared against come from Kapur 2000 with [11C]raclopride
(`CLINICAL_OCCUPANCY_THRESHOLDS`). Neither P22.4 nor ledger row V11 says this.
Restricted to the two same-tracer candidates, the outcome does not change:
Uchida's free-Emax fit cannot reach 65% at any concentration, and the
Emax-fixed fit needs 897 ng/mL, 1.50 times the top of the effective range.
Both give Outcome A. The margin under the like-for-like tracer is 1.50x, not
2.94x, and both raclopride rows are second-hand. The pre-registration chose
the striatal (putamen) row as primary and C1 forbids changing that choice
afterwards, so the headline stays as it is. The ledger row now carries the
caveat. The Emax-convention spread between the two raclopride rows (4.6x) is
larger than the tracer question in any case.

**2. The pk layer is the bottleneck, and its search was narrower than the
records make it look.** Seven of nine drugs stop there. P21.3 searched only
FDA review documents, on the premise that they carry the analyses journals
put behind paywalls. Its searched-and-empty rows (clozapine, quetiapine and
oral aripiprazole not-fitted; paliperidone redacted) are true of the FDA
corpus. They are not a statement about the published literature, and no task
in this repository has searched that literature for an oral adult
population-PK model of any of these drugs. This is the cheapest repair in the
matrix with the largest effect. It is an untested route, not an empty one.

**3. The Kp,uu gate is hidden behind the pk refusal** (see the grid notes).
This is a recording gap in P22.2's refusal attribution, not a data gap.

**4. The occupancy layer is not the weak point it could have been.** The
hyperbola with someone else's constant passes when regimen and tracer match
(V9) and misses when they do not (V10, and haloperidol's split D). That
pattern points to the regimen and to between-study spread in published
constants, not to the functional form. With one ziprasidone subject it is a
pattern, not a finding.

## What passes, and what the passes are worth

- **V8 (label dose).** Both scored drugs pass. Dose scales exactly with EC50
  in this chain, so olanzapine would pass for any EC50 between 0.73x and 2.90x
  of de Greef's value, and haloperidol between 0.48x and 1.93x of V1's. Both
  bands are about as wide as the 2-2.5x tracer and region floor, and
  haloperidol's scored label range spans 15-fold. The passes show the untuned
  chain lands on clinically used doses. They cannot rank constants.
- **V9 (aripiprazole).** The cleanest comparison in the ledger: same tracer,
  same regimen class, disjoint subjects. It does not exercise the chain,
  because aripiprazole has no oral PK and is refused at the analyte layer.

## Repair order

1. **Search the journal literature for oral adult population-PK models** for
   clozapine, ziprasidone, quetiapine, paliperidone and amisulpride. Record
   every candidate as accepted or refused under `fda_pk_bridge`'s criteria
   (typical values, units, reference weight, parent-only analyte). This is the
   only repair that could raise the number of drivable drugs without new
   measurement.
2. **Enter a `not-required` Kp,uu record together with any drug that gets
   PK**, and add the exposure gate to `refusal_layers` so the attribution is
   complete.
3. **Read the closed tables** named in `SEARCHED_AND_EMPTY`, first-hand
   (Nordström 1995, Kapur 1999 and Kessler 2006 for clozapine come first,
   because they would also give a first-hand striatal raclopride constant).
   This is the only route to a held-out test for seven of nine drugs.
4. **Regimen-matched steady-state oral PET** for ziprasidone, before reading
   V10 as anything more than one subject.
5. **Only then** the layers no data can currently repair: effect-site
   kinetics (no human ke0), partial-agonist transduction (no intrinsic
   activity exists), and the occupancy-to-outcome link, which V11 has already
   falsified as a sole mediator and Phase 23 is meant to address.

## Standing limitations, unchanged

Every limitation P19.7 lists still applies to each drug the chain drives, and
none was relaxed to produce a number here. In particular: no basis conversion
as a rescue (V2), no tracer pooling, no in-vitro Ki as an in-vivo EC50, and no
reading of an occupancy as a clinical outcome.
