# P9.2: sparse Jacobian and stiff-solver comparison

The implementation is in [`circuit_forward.py`](../src/neurocascade/calibration/circuit_forward.py).
`circuit_jacobian()` differentiates the exact Wilson–Cowan RHS, including the
sigmoid clipping rails, and returns a CSR matrix. `steady_state_rates()` passes
that Jacobian and its structural sparsity pattern to SciPy's BDF and Radau
methods. RK45 remains the default for compatibility and because the active
whole-brain workload does not show a general implicit-solver win.

The benchmark is [`benchmark_stiff_solver.py`](../scripts/benchmark_stiff_solver.py).
It uses five target layers (`D2`, `GABA_A_BZD`, `MOR`, `M1`, `NMDA`) at all
three supported resolutions, and measures both the drug-free and active
steady-state paths. The active input is `x = 0.1` per target, solely as a
repeatable numerical workload. Results are in
[`p9_2_stiff_solver.csv`](../results/benchmarks/p9_2_stiff_solver.csv) and
[`p9_2_stiff_solver.json`](../results/benchmarks/p9_2_stiff_solver.json).

## Accuracy and timing

Seconds on the recorded Linux/Python 3.12 environment; errors are maximum
absolute rate differences from RK45 at `rtol=1e-8`, `atol=1e-11`.

| Resolution | Workload | RK45 | BDF + sparse J | Radau + sparse J | BDF error | Radau error |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| regions (291) | baseline | 0.167 | 0.096 | 1.224 | 3.4e-08 | 3.4e-08 |
| regions (291) | active | 0.944 | 1.072 | 14.450 | 7.3e-11 | 6.1e-10 |
| subdivisions (60) | baseline | 0.041 | 0.031 | 0.041 | 6.7e-09 | 6.7e-09 |
| subdivisions (60) | active | 0.282 | 0.223 | 0.566 | 1.4e-10 | 4.1e-10 |
| major divisions (12) | baseline | 0.008 | 0.011 | 0.016 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| major divisions (12) | active | 0.042 | 0.092 | 0.155 | 1.6e-09 | 7.9e-10 |

Both implicit methods preserve the reference to substantially tighter than the
model's fixed-point tolerance. BDF is faster for the baseline and
60-subdivision active workloads, but slower for the important 291-region
active workload and for the 12-node active workload. Radau is slower in every
active case. Therefore no default is switched: callers can request
`method="BDF"` or `method="Radau"` when their workload is known to benefit,
while the established RK45 path remains the measured general-purpose choice.

The Jacobian is also exposed through the held-out and single-target whole-brain
model `rates()` methods, so the opt-in solver is available without rebuilding
the drive or connection-modulation dictionaries.
